01 · Understand the service

What Is Swedish Massage?

Swedish massage is a general wellness massage commonly using long gliding strokes, kneading and comfortable, adjustable pressure. It is intended to support relaxation—not to diagnose or treat a medical condition.

Gentle, flowing strokes

The therapist begins with broad, comfortable movements and checks pressure before working more specifically on areas the customer has agreed to.

A personalised wellness session

Duration, focus areas, positioning and pressure are agreed during consultation. The customer can ask for an adjustment or stop at any time.

02 · Potential wellness benefits

Benefits of Swedish Massage

People respond differently. These are possible short-term wellness benefits, not guaranteed outcomes or medical claims.

Relaxation and stress relief

A quiet, comfortable session may help the customer feel calmer and more relaxed.

Everyday muscle tension

Gentle massage may temporarily ease mild tightness associated with normal daily activity.

Comfort and body awareness

Slower, mindful touch can help customers notice their comfort and pressure preferences.

Restful wellness routine

A relaxing treatment may complement healthy rest and self-care habits.

03 · Personalise the experience

Massage Oils to Choose From

Use only suitable massage products, check ingredient sensitivities and ask the customer before applying fragrance. Oil selection depends on availability and skin compatibility.

Unscented Massage Oil

A fragrance-free option for customers who prefer to avoid added scent.

Sweet Almond-Based Oil

May provide smooth glide. Avoid if the customer has a relevant nut allergy or sensitivity.

Lightly Scented Oil

Only by preference and when suitable; fragrance can be skipped if it causes irritation or discomfort.

Always ask about allergies, asthma triggers, pregnancy-related sensitivities and skin reactions. Essential oils must never be applied undiluted; skip scented products when unsure.

05 · Suitability

Who May Find It Helpful?

Swedish massage is commonly chosen for general relaxation and everyday muscular tightness when the person is otherwise well and the treatment is appropriate.

Desk-work stiffness

May offer temporary comfort for ordinary shoulder or back tension. New, severe or unexplained pain needs assessment first.

Relaxation after everyday activity

Can be part of a balanced relaxation routine; it is not a substitute for injury assessment or rehabilitation.

06 · Read before booking

Contraindications & Safety Precautions

Tell the therapist about relevant health conditions, medication concerns, recent procedures, injuries and any symptoms that feel unusual.

When to avoid, postpone or seek medical advice

  • Suspected blood clot: do not massage the affected area; seek urgent medical assessment.
  • Fever or acute illness: postpone the session until recovered.
  • Open wounds, contagious skin conditions or inflamed skin: avoid the affected area; postpone if needed.
  • Recent surgery, fracture or significant injury: obtain appropriate medical clearance before massage.
  • Severe, unexplained or rapidly worsening pain, new numbness or weakness: seek medical assessment rather than using massage to treat the symptom.
  • Pregnancy, bleeding disorders, blood-thinning medication or significant medical conditions: discuss suitability with a qualified healthcare professional and use a suitably trained therapist.

Stop immediately if sharp pain, dizziness, numbness, breathing difficulty or unusual discomfort occurs. Do not apply deep pressure over bones, the front of the neck, injured tissue or areas the customer has not consented to.

07 · Technique guide

Key Strokes & Techniques

A trained therapist selects appropriate techniques based on consent, comfort, training and the customer’s needs. Deep or painful pressure is not required for Swedish massage.

TECHNIQUE 01 · FLOW

Effleurage — Long Gliding Strokes

The therapist uses broad, smooth strokes to introduce touch, spread suitable massage oil and build a calm rhythm. Pressure is light at first and adjusted only after the customer confirms comfort.

Professional cue: keep the hand contact smooth and controlled; avoid dragging over dry skin, bony areas or irritated tissue.

TECHNIQUE 02 · KNEAD

Petrissage — Gentle Kneading

Where appropriate, trained hands gently compress and release suitable muscle tissue, commonly around the shoulders or back. It should never feel pinching, bruising or sharp.

Professional cue: ask for feedback, use only pressure within training and avoid recent injuries or areas with unexplained pain.

TECHNIQUE 03 · COMFORT

Gentle Circular Movements

Small, controlled movements may be used on suitable muscle areas to vary the sensation without suddenly increasing pressure. The customer can ask to skip any technique.

Professional cue: do not press directly on the spine, joints, visible inflammation or any area the customer has not agreed to include.

TECHNIQUE 04 · FINISH

Closing Strokes & Comfort Check

Gradually slow and lighten the strokes so the session ends calmly. Give the customer time to sit up, check how they feel and offer simple aftercare guidance.

Professional cue: stop if discomfort, dizziness, numbness or another unwanted symptom appears.

10 · Therapist workflow

Step-by-Step Massage Process

A practical service sequence for trained therapists. Adapt it to the client, the agreed service and professional scope; do not treat this guide as medical training.

STEP 01 · CONSULT

Consult, Screen & Obtain Consent

Ask about the customer’s goals, focus areas, pressure preferences, allergies, relevant health conditions, recent injury or surgery, and areas to avoid. Explain the session and obtain clear consent. Postpone or refer if a concern makes massage unsuitable.

STEP 02 · PREPARE

Prepare the Room & Equipment

Wash or sanitise hands as appropriate, clean contact surfaces, prepare fresh linens and check the table or bed for stability. Confirm the room is comfortable and that suitable products and waste disposal are ready.

STEP 03 · POSITION

Position Comfortably & Explain Draping

Explain the position before assisting the customer. Provide privacy to change, use appropriate draping and uncover only the agreed area. Ask whether the head, neck, shoulders or any other area should be excluded.

STEP 04 · WARM UP

Start with Gentle Gliding Strokes

Apply an appropriate amount of suitable massage medium only after checking sensitivities. Begin with slow, light effleurage on the agreed area and ask whether the pressure, pace and temperature feel comfortable.

STEP 05 · TREAT

Follow the Agreed Massage Sequence

Use appropriate gliding strokes and gentle kneading within professional training. Keep movements controlled, adapt to the customer’s response and avoid bones, injured tissue, inflamed areas and anything not consented to.

STEP 06 · CHECK

Check Pressure & Comfort Throughout

Check in naturally rather than assuming the pressure is right. Reduce pressure or stop immediately if the customer asks, or if pain, numbness, dizziness, breathing difficulty or unusual discomfort occurs.

STEP 07 · CLOSE

Finish Gradually & Give Aftercare Guidance

Slow and lighten the strokes, let the customer know the massage is ending and allow them time to sit up. Ask how they feel and share simple comfort guidance without promising a medical result.

STEP 08 · RESET

Clean, Reset & Record Relevant Notes

Dispose of single-use items safely, replace linens and clean reusable equipment before the next appointment. Record service details and relevant customer feedback according to company policy and privacy requirements.
